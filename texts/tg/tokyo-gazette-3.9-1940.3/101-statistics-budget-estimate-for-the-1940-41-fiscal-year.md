# BUDGET ESTIMATES FOR THE 1940-41 FISCAL YEAR

DEPARTMENT OF FINANCE

WHEN the new Cabinet was formed under the leadership of Admiral Yonai during the new year recess of the Imperial Diet, public interest was aroused, in no small measure, as to how matters concerning the budget estimates would be disposed of. The Yonai Cabinet, however, lost no time in deciding to adopt the estimates drafted by its predecessor, the Abe Cabinet, after thorough examination and study, and to introduce them to the Diet. The 1940-41 estimates now being deliberated on, therefore, are those decided on by the Abe Cabinet, in respect to the General Expenditures, on December 8, 1939, and in respect to the Emergency Military Expenditures, on January 6, 1940.

In presenting an outline of the 1940-41 budget estimates, however, the 3rd supplementary budget for the Special Account for Emergency Military Expenditures will also be taken up from time to time, since it is inseparably related to the general budget.

### Principles Adopted in Compiling the Budget

The principles on which the budget for the next fiscal year was formulated was originally established by the Hiranuma Cabinet, on July 4, 1939. When the Abe Cabinet succeeded to that of Baron Hiranuma, it was decided to adhere to the same principles, the major points of which are as follows:

1. In respect to demands for appropriations to cover new undertakings, special attention shall be paid to the selection of items so as to limit such demands only to those that are really essential and urgent in view of the current situation. At the same time, the size of the appropriations to meet such demands shall be kept to the minimum.

2. A close re-examination shall be made of the appropriations intended to cover undertakings already authorized, with a view to exercising further economies by such means as the lowering of standards, the increasing of efficiency, and the postponement of new enterprises.

3. Particular care shall be taken for the maintenance of harmony with the various economic policies concerning materials, labour,

[[p. 334]]

funds and prices, lest the operation of the wartime economy be hampered.

4. The taxation system shall be revised with the object of increasing tax receipts, while at the same time efforts shall be made to obtain more revenue from other ordinary items. Further, means shall be devised for transferring as much as is feasible from the various special accounts to the Special Account for Emergency Military Expenditures or the General account.

5. Statements of the budget estimates of all the Departments shall be submitted to the Department of Finance not later than August 10, 1939, in the case of the General Account and by August 31, the same year, in the case of the special accounts.

Briefly, principles adopted in compiling the 1940-41 estimates have been to aim at (1) increase in ordinary revenue, and (2) at curtailment of expenditures, with a view to working out the most adequate wartime budget. Another important point to be noted is that the Department of Finance, deliberating on the estimates for the General Account, had always to take them up along with the requirements of the Emergency Militaty Expenditures, in order to get a comprehensive picture of the supply and demand of materials and other related matters. The procedure for drafting the 1940-41 estimates was naturally very complicated; but the fact that the Cabinet was able to render a decision on this matter with such smoothness and dispatch, was a source of gratification to the authorities concerned.

In addition to the afore-mentioned items, a Special Account for Taxes to be Allocated to Local Governments and Municipalities as revenue has been created to replace the Special Grants to Local Finances which will be abolished when a reform of the local taxation system is carried out. The taxes to be allocated to local

[[p. 336]]

governments and municipalities, excepting those collected direct, in the said Special Account, are to be transferred from the General Account to the Special Account. Accordingly, a sum of 277,355,000 yen has been included in the general expenditures.

Again, a total of 600,000,000 yen out of the estimated increases in tax receipts on the basis of the taxation reform carried out since the 1938-39 fiscal year and in estimated profits to be gained by the Monopoly Bureau by the raising of tobacco prices, etc. has been earmarked for transfer to the Special Account for Emergency Military Expenditures. The two items of transfer referred to above, namely those relating to taxes to be allocated to prefectures and municipalities and those relating to emergency military expenditures, are both what may be termed transit accounts. If these are excluded, total expenditures will come to 4,945,606,000 yen instead of 5,822,962,000 yen.

Much effort has also been directed toward curtailing expenditures previously authorized for various undertakings. Accordingly, such undertakings as require key commodities will be deferred wherever possible, to say nothing of non-essential and non-urgent undertakings, besides the lowering of standards and devising other means of retrenchment.

As is clear from the foregoing table, the greater part of the estimated increases of normal revenue is accounted for by an estimated increase in tax receipts, an increase which is based on the proposed taxation reform. It should be noticed in this connection, that the proposed taxation reform is to realize the equitable distribution of the tax burden, to try to harmonize the taxation system with the various economic measures to be carried out by the Government, and to plan for an increase in tax receipts, as well as to render the tax system as elastic and as simple as possible.
