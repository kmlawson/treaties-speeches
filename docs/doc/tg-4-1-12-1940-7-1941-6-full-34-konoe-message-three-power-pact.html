Message of the Prime Minister (Prince Fumimaro Konoe) on the Conclusion of the Three-Power Pact

Type
Speech
Date
27 September 1940
Speaker / issued by
Prince Fumimaro Konoe, Prime Minister
Printed in
Tokyo Gazette, November, 1940, pp. 194-195
Section
Documents Concerning the Three-Power Pact
Transcription note: Date taken from the context (Pact signed September 27, 1940); not printed with the message.
View the scan on the Internet Archive →
p. 194

On the occasion of the conclusion of the Three-Power Pact between Japan, Germany and Italy, an Imperial Rescript has been issued, setting forth clearly the aims of our Empire and pointing out the path which our nation should follow. I am greatly moved by the boundless benevolence of our Sovereign.

To maintain world peace and to insure the stability of East Asia is indeed the immutable national policy of Japan, firmly rooted in the principles on which our Empire was founded.

With the outbreak of war in Europe last autumn, conflict and confusion have spread increasingly over the world. It is an urgent necessity of this moment to devise measures for the cessation of the disturbances and the restoration of peace. Germany and Italy share with our Empire in the same ideals and aspirations. Accordingly, with a view to joining hands with these Powers to establish a new order in the respective regions, and furthermore to cooperating with them toward the restoration of world peace, Japan has now concluded a Three-Power Pact. The time has come for our nation to proceed with fresh resolve to construct a new order in Greater East Asia.

However, to carry out fully the conviction of our country and to enable all nations to find each its proper place is indeed a difficult task. The goal lies far ahead. We must expect to encounter numerous obstacles as we go on.

p. 195

I confidently hope that, in obedience to the Imperial will, our people, in order to surmount the present emergency, will make clear the concept of national polity, unite heart and soul, and overcome any and every obstacle, thereby setting at ease the august mind of our Sovereign.

About this collection

This site gathers the verbatim texts of treaties, laws, speeches and other official documents printed in two English-language periodicals published in wartime Japan: the Tokyo Gazette (1937–1942), a monthly digest of government reports issued under the supervision of the Cabinet Information Bureau, and Contemporary Japan: A Review of Far Eastern Affairs (issues of 1941–1945), published by the Foreign Affairs Association of Japan, whose “Documentary Material” section reprinted official texts.

Only texts printed in full in the journals are included; commentary, summaries and articles about them are not. The issues covered are those indexed on the Tokyo Gazette table of contents and Contemporary Japan pages at Frog in a Well, and scanned on the Internet Archive.

These are official translations and statements of the wartime Japanese state and its allies, and should be read as such.

Transcription. The texts were transcribed from the page scans by a large language model reading the page images, and have not been proofread by a person. Original spelling and romanisation are kept. Page numbers in the margin link to the page of the scan on the Internet Archive: check the scan before quoting.
