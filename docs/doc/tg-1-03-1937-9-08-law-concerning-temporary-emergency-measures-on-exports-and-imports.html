Law

Law Concerning Temporary Emergency Measures on Exports and Imports

Type
Law
Date
10 September 1937
Author
Bureau of Information, Prime Minister's Department
Printed in
Tokyo Gazette, September, 1937, pp. 46-47
Section
Emergency Legislative Measures Incidental to the China Affair
Transcription note: Official summary, not the text of the law. (Law No. 92, 1937, Promulgated on and Enforced since September 10th)
View the scan on the Internet Archive →
p. 46

With the development of the China Affair, enormous quantities of goods will be required for military and defence purposes. To ensure an abundant

p. 47

and smooth supply of these materials and articles, it is imperative, in view of the actual condition of national resources, that exports should be restricted and imports controlled more or less not only in the case of those materials and articles which are comparatively unnecessary or less urgently needed but also even where they are needed for industrial purposes. The Government is, accordingly, empowered, when deemed necessary, to prohibit or restrict exports or imports of goods in accordance with the provisions of this Law (Article 1).

As it is possible that the application of these emergency measures may entail a sharp rise in the prices or uncertainty in the supply of the materials and articles affected by the legislation, the Government is empowered to take such remedial steps as are deemed advisable. This will enable the Government, in pursuance of the orders to be issued under this Law, to do the necessary in regard to the production of articles having as their materials the goods just mentioned, or in regard to restricting their manufacture, or to issuing orders concerning the distribution, transfer, employment or consumption of the said goods or of manufactures having these goods for their materials (Article 2). This Law shall be annulled within one year after the cessation of the China Affair (Addenda).

About this collection

This site gathers the verbatim texts of treaties, laws, speeches and other official documents printed in two English-language periodicals published in wartime Japan: the Tokyo Gazette (1937–1942), a monthly digest of government reports issued under the supervision of the Cabinet Information Bureau, and Contemporary Japan: A Review of Far Eastern Affairs (issues of 1941–1945), published by the Foreign Affairs Association of Japan, whose “Documentary Material” section reprinted official texts.

Only texts printed in full in the journals are included; commentary, summaries and articles about them are not. The issues covered are those indexed on the Tokyo Gazette table of contents and Contemporary Japan pages at Frog in a Well, and scanned on the Internet Archive.

These are official translations and statements of the wartime Japanese state and its allies, and should be read as such.

Transcription. The texts were transcribed from the page scans by a large language model reading the page images, and have not been proofread by a person. Original spelling and romanisation are kept. Page numbers in the margin link to the page of the scan on the Internet Archive: check the scan before quoting.
