Mr. Koki Hirota's Note to Sir Robert L. Craigie, Dated December 14, 1937 (The Ladybird Incident)

Type
Document
Date
14 December 1937
Speaker / issued by
Koki Hirota, Minister for Foreign Affairs of Japan
Printed in
Tokyo Gazette, January, 1938, pp. 20-21
Section
Documents concerning the Panay and Ladybird Incidents; II. The Ladybird Incident
Transcription note: Addressed to Sir Robert L. Craigie, British Ambassador in Tokyo. Printed without salutation or signature.
View the scan on the Internet Archive →
p. 20

I have the honour to state that the Japanese Government regrets profoundly the incidents of December 12th, in which British men-of-war,

p. 21

the Ladybird, the Bee, the Cricket and the Scarab, were by mistake subjected to gunfire and aerial bombing by Japanese forces in the vicinities of Wuhu and Nanking, and to express herewith to Your Excellency on behalf of my Government its sincere apologies. I desire to inform Your Excellency that the Japanese Government has already taken the necessary steps to prevent the recurrence of an incident of such character, and to add that it will, upon the completion of investigations, deal appropriately with those responsible for the incidents, and also that it is prepared to make the necessary indemnification for the losses sustained by the British.

It is the fervent hope of the Japanese Government that the traditional friendship between Japan and Great Britain will not be affected by these unfortunate incidents.

About this collection

This site gathers the verbatim texts of treaties, laws, speeches and other official documents printed in two English-language periodicals published in wartime Japan: the Tokyo Gazette (1937–1942), a monthly digest of government reports issued under the supervision of the Cabinet Information Bureau, and Contemporary Japan: A Review of Far Eastern Affairs (issues of 1941–1945), published by the Foreign Affairs Association of Japan, whose “Documentary Material” section reprinted official texts.

Only texts printed in full in the journals are included; commentary, summaries and articles about them are not. The issues covered are those indexed on the Tokyo Gazette table of contents and Contemporary Japan pages at Frog in a Well, and scanned on the Internet Archive.

These are official translations and statements of the wartime Japanese state and its allies, and should be read as such.

Transcription. The texts were transcribed from the page scans by a large language model reading the page images, and have not been proofread by a person. Original spelling and romanisation are kept. Page numbers in the margin link to the page of the scan on the Internet Archive: check the scan before quoting.
