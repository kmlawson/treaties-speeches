The Government of the Empire of Japan and the Royal Government of Thailand,

Being unshakably determined, in close co-operation between the two countries, to prosecute the common war against the United States of America and the British Empire to its successful conclusion and to establish a greater East Asia based on justice, have agreed upon the following articles:

Article 1. Japan recognizes the incorporation into the territories of Thailand of the States of Kelantan, Trengganu, Kedah and Perlis and the islands belonging to those States.

Article 2. Japan recognizes the incorporation into the territories of Thailand of the States of Kentung and Mong Pan in the Shan region.

Article 3. Japan will cease the administration which it at present exercises in the territories specified in the two foregoing articles within sixty days from the date of coming into force of the present treaty.

Article 4. The frontiers of the territories specified in the Articles 1 and 2 shall be in accordance with the boundaries of the States as at the date of the signing of the present treaty.

Article 5. The matters of detail for the execution of the present treaty shall be decided through consultations between the authorities concerned of the two countries.

Article 6. The present treaty shall come into force on the date of its signature.

Transcription note

Printed within the Board of Information announcement; no separate title for the treaty. Signed at Bangkok by Teiji Tsubokami and Field Marshal P. Phibul Songgram.

About this collection

This site gathers the verbatim texts of treaties, laws, speeches and other official documents printed in two English-language periodicals published in wartime Japan: the Tokyo Gazette (1937–1942), a monthly digest of government reports issued under the supervision of the Cabinet Information Bureau, and Contemporary Japan: A Review of Far Eastern Affairs (issues of 1941–1945), published by the Foreign Affairs Association of Japan, whose “Documentary Material” section reprinted official texts.

It also includes the “Texts of Treaties, Agreements, Notes and Statutes of the Powers Concerning Manchuria” (1895–1932) printed as Supplement III of the Japan-Manchoukuo Year Book 1934 (the first of a section of texts from year books), and, from the two periodicals, selected government programmes, reports, statistical articles, chronologies and profiles of public figures.

Only texts printed in full in the journals are included; commentary, summaries and articles about them are not. The issues covered are those indexed on the Tokyo Gazette table of contents and Contemporary Japan pages at Frog in a Well, and scanned on the Internet Archive.

These are official translations and statements of the wartime Japanese state and its allies, and should be read as such.
