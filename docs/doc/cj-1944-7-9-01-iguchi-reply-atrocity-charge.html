Japan's Reply on Atrocity Charge to the United States: Statement by Sadao Iguchi, the Board of Information, May 8, 1944

Date

Speaker / issued by

Printed in

Section
Documentary Material
View the scan on the Internet Archive →
p. 834

It is scarcely necessary to say that the Japanese Government have, from the humanitarian viewpoint, done their best in order that all enemy nationals, both prisoners of war and civilian internees, under Japanese control may be given as fair and just treatment as possible. Nevertheless, under the date of February 5, the United States Government lodged a protest with the Japanese Government regarding the treatment of Americans under their control and, at the same time, widely propagandized Japan's alleged maltreatment of enemy nationals.

As the result of most careful and detailed investigations concerning the various charges made by the United States Government, a lengthy reply, setting forth clearly how American nationals are being actually treated by the Japanese authorities and pointing out minutely the errors and misunderstandings on the part of the United States Government was handed to the Swiss Minister in Tokyo, who represents the protecting power of American interests, on May 3. The Japanese Government, took the occasion to call the attention of the United States Government to the unjust treatment accorded to Japanese naitonals [sic] by the American authorities, enumerating concrete cases as reported from reliable sources, and to demand the rectification thereof.

At the same time, the Japanese Government declared their readiness to comply with the request of the United States Government and dispatch a Japanese vessel to Vladivostok in order to take over and transport the relief supplies and mail matters which are to be shipped via that port from the United States to American war prisoners and civilian internees under the control of Japan. A definite plan for that purpose was communicated through the Swiss Minister to the United States Government.

About this collection

This site gathers the verbatim texts of treaties, laws, speeches and other official documents printed in two English-language periodicals published in wartime Japan: the Tokyo Gazette (1937–1942), a monthly digest of government reports issued under the supervision of the Cabinet Information Bureau, and Contemporary Japan: A Review of Far Eastern Affairs (issues of 1941–1945), published by the Foreign Affairs Association of Japan, whose “Documentary Material” section reprinted official texts.

It also includes the “Texts of Treaties, Agreements, Notes and Statutes of the Powers Concerning Manchuria” (1895–1932) printed as Supplement III of the Japan-Manchoukuo Year Book 1934, and, from the two periodicals, selected government programmes, reports, statistical articles, chronologies and profiles of public figures.

Only texts printed in full in the journals are included; commentary, summaries and articles about them are not. The issues covered are those indexed on the Tokyo Gazette table of contents and Contemporary Japan pages at Frog in a Well, and scanned on the Internet Archive.

These are official translations and statements of the wartime Japanese state and its allies, and should be read as such.
