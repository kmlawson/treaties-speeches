p. 28

I have the honour on instructions from His Majesty's Government in the United Kingdom to inform Your Excellency that it has noted with appreciation the assurances contained in Your Excellency's note of December 28th in connexion with the attacks on British warships and merchant shipping on December 12th, and has learnt with satisfaction that the statements contained in your note of December 14th apply to merchant vessels concerned as well as to warships.

His Majesty's Government is bound to observe that its information in regard to the circumstances in which the attacks took place—notably, for instance, on points of visibility—is at variance with that of the Japanese Government.

His Majesty's Government notes, however, with satisfaction that the Japanese Government has taken or is prepared to take the necessary measures to deal suitably with the officers responsible for these incidents and to prevent any repetition. As regards the latter His Majesty's Government considers that the details of these measures and their effective application may suitably form the subject of further conversations in the course of which they do not doubt that they will be informed of actual steps decided upon.

About this collection

This site gathers the verbatim texts of treaties, laws, speeches and other official documents printed in two English-language periodicals published in wartime Japan: the Tokyo Gazette (1937–1942), a monthly digest of government reports issued under the supervision of the Cabinet Information Bureau, and Contemporary Japan: A Review of Far Eastern Affairs (issues of 1941–1945), published by the Foreign Affairs Association of Japan, whose “Documentary Material” section reprinted official texts.

It also includes the “Texts of Treaties, Agreements, Notes and Statutes of the Powers Concerning Manchuria” (1895–1932) printed as Supplement III of the Japan-Manchoukuo Year Book 1934, and, from the two periodicals, selected government programmes, reports, statistical articles, chronologies and profiles of public figures.

Only texts printed in full in the journals are included; commentary, summaries and articles about them are not. The issues covered are those indexed on the Tokyo Gazette table of contents and Contemporary Japan pages at Frog in a Well, and scanned on the Internet Archive.

These are official translations and statements of the wartime Japanese state and its allies, and should be read as such.
