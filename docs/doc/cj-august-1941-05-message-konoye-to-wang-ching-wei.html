p. 1104

The recent Cabinet change was effected to render more vigorous the execution of national policies in meeting the world situation by perfecting and strengthening with speed the internal structure of our country. The fact that a renovation has been effected in the composition of the Cabinet through the change is as Your Excellency knows through our Government announcements and others.

There is, of course, no change whatever in Japan's foreign policy, and our fixed policy toward your country and the close and cordial relations between the two countries, which have further been strengthened through Your Excellency's recent visit to Japan, will remain unaffected in the least. I earnestly hope that Your Excellency will take note of this fact and continue to exert your valiant efforts for the stabilization of East Aaia [sic]. As for my part, I wish to add that, in accordance with the pledge made between Your Excellency and myself, I will continue to render with increasing effort a hearty co-operation for the collaboration between your country and mine and for the prosperity of China.

Transcription note

Item (1) of two. Announced by the Board of Information on July 23, 1941; the date of the message itself is not given. The introduction says the messages were exchanged upon the formation of the third Konoye Cabinet, "renewing thereby the determination of Japan and China to march bravely forward for the establishment of the new order in East Asia". "East Aaia" is the source's misprint.

About this collection

This site gathers the verbatim texts of treaties, laws, speeches and other official documents printed in two English-language periodicals published in wartime Japan: the Tokyo Gazette (1937–1942), a monthly digest of government reports issued under the supervision of the Cabinet Information Bureau, and Contemporary Japan: A Review of Far Eastern Affairs (issues of 1941–1945), published by the Foreign Affairs Association of Japan, whose “Documentary Material” section reprinted official texts.

Only texts printed in full in the journals are included; commentary, summaries and articles about them are not. The issues covered are those indexed on the Tokyo Gazette table of contents and Contemporary Japan pages at Frog in a Well, and scanned on the Internet Archive.

These are official translations and statements of the wartime Japanese state and its allies, and should be read as such.
