WHOLESALE AND RETAIL TRADE IN THE BIG SIX CITIES1

—As Shown By the Census of Materials—

CABINET INFORMATION BUREAU

RETURNS of the census taken of materials on August 1, 1939, have been published by the Cabinet Bureau of Statistics, although the publication contains only a part of the returns, being limited to those relating to the kinds of business and the number of stores by types of business operation. This kind of census, which has seldom been attempted in other countries, has furnished valuable data for establishing the war-time economic structure, by showing distinctly through what channels the daily necessaries of the people are distributed.

In this census the different kinds of trades are classified into 48 categories according to the articles dealt in; such as cereals, confectionery and bread, drinks and condiments, footgear and native umbrellas, firewood and charcoal, secondhand articles, etc. The stores are classified into 11 groups according to the types of business in which they are engaged; such as department store, retail trade, producers' retail trade, wholesale and retail trade, street-stall, wholesale trade, foreign trade, producers' cooperatives, consumers' cooperatives, other types of joint sale and purchase, and brokers. The detailed figures given for each of these items throw a revealing light on certain phases of the commercial system in this country. In the number of stores, Tokyo stands, as might be expected, away at the head of the list, with 219,887, Osaka coming next with 121,306. A similar disparity is seen in the number of retail stores, the figure for Tokyo being 133,444 in contrast to 70,583 for Osaka. In the ratio of stores to the total number of families in each city, however, Nagoya comes first and foremost with 191 stores per thousand families; followed by Kyoto with 180 stores; Osaka with 179; Kobe with 170; Tokyo with 167; and Yokohama with 166.

About this collection

This site gathers the verbatim texts of treaties, laws, speeches and other official documents printed in two English-language periodicals published in wartime Japan: the Tokyo Gazette (1937–1942), a monthly digest of government reports issued under the supervision of the Cabinet Information Bureau, and Contemporary Japan: A Review of Far Eastern Affairs (issues of 1941–1945), published by the Foreign Affairs Association of Japan, whose “Documentary Material” section reprinted official texts.

Only texts printed in full in the journals are included; commentary, summaries and articles about them are not. The issues covered are those indexed on the Tokyo Gazette table of contents and Contemporary Japan pages at Frog in a Well, and scanned on the Internet Archive.

These are official translations and statements of the wartime Japanese state and its allies, and should be read as such.
