Programme for Mobilization of Materials for 1940

Board of Planning

p. 107

TAKING into consideration the changes in the international situation and other circumstances, a temporary programme for the mobilization of goods was enforced from April to June, while a standard plan for mobilization of materials for the whole year of 1940 was drafted with all speed. The latter was decided upon in conjunction with another relating to foreign trade at a Cabinet meeting held on June 29. The Materials Mobilization plan may be considered the basis upon which Japan's national economy is being made to operate, since it provides for the controlling and regulating of the supply and distribution of as many as two hundred and fifty varieties of the more essential goods. Its application is indeed of the greatest consequence in the execution of national policy and in its effect upon the people's livelihood.

General Condition of Supply of Materials

When we examine into the conditions surrounding the supply of materials, we find that their source lies mostly in domestic production, and where a deficiency exists, this may be complemented by imports from Manchoukuo and China, or from such countries as Great Britain and the United States. Despite the fact that domestic production was faced with many obstacles last year due to the drought and shortage of coal and electric power, the actual returns for this year may safely be expected to be more favourable in keeping with the greater progress made in the execution of the plan for the expansion of productive activity since 1938. The unprecedented dryness and other adverse influences completely negatived in certain localities the increase that had been made in industrial equipment with that end in view. Nevertheless the actual results achieved, save in certain exceptional cases, were a distinct advance upon the previous year. As various measures have since been taken to counteract any unforeseen calamities, and as a considerable number of factories which had been under construction have now been completed and are in operation, a further augmentation in production may be counted upon during 1940.

A considerable increase in the volume of imports from Man-

choukuo and China can also be expected, as 1939 was indeed a difficult year for those countries affected as they were by drought and flood over large areas. The European war, which has brought about an inordinate rise in prices generally, and the policies adopted by the belligerents and several neutral nations prohibiting or restricting the exportation of various classes of goods in view of the requirements of national defence have removed all opportunities for purchases by Japan in these markets. Imports from these foreign countries therefore can hardly be expected to rise.

General Condition of Demand

It is clear that under the present situation, the repletion of armaments by Japan, the expansion of productive power, construction work on the Asiatic continent and a number of national undertakings of vital importance are giving rise to unusual demands. The quantity of materials and goods required in connection with each of these undertakings is considerable, and more so when the trend of the world situation revolving around the European war is taken into consideration. Under these circumstances, it is only by a proper adjustment of supply to demand that the maximum utility can be obtained out of the materials at hand. And for this reason the basic principle to follow resides in the expedient distribution of essential requirements.

Basic Policy Governing the 1940 Plan

It is in such a spirit that the materials mobilization plan for 1940 has been formulated with a view to accomplishing the object of the China Affair while assuring in every way the defence of the country by such measures as will meet every new development in the international situation. The plan resolves within itself the attainment of these five main objectives : namely, (1) the meeting of every demand for military supplies, (2) the expansion of productive power to the fullest extent, (3) the early completion of constructive undertakings on the continent, embracing Manchoukuo and China, (4) the augmentation of importing capacity, with special emphasis on the promotion of export trade, (5) the assurance of the minimum essentials for the livelihood of the people.

About this collection

This site gathers the verbatim texts of treaties, laws, speeches and other official documents printed in two English-language periodicals published in wartime Japan: the Tokyo Gazette (1937–1942), a monthly digest of government reports issued under the supervision of the Cabinet Information Bureau, and Contemporary Japan: A Review of Far Eastern Affairs (issues of 1941–1945), published by the Foreign Affairs Association of Japan, whose “Documentary Material” section reprinted official texts.

Only texts printed in full in the journals are included; commentary, summaries and articles about them are not. The issues covered are those indexed on the Tokyo Gazette table of contents and Contemporary Japan pages at Frog in a Well, and scanned on the Internet Archive.

These are official translations and statements of the wartime Japanese state and its allies, and should be read as such.
