I

Announcement by the Board of Information Made on November 6, 1941

The passenger ship, Kehi Maru, of the Nippon Kai Steamship Company, which is on the regular run between Turuga and Seisin, met with a disaster, shortly after 10:00 p.m. last night, on the Japan Sea by contacting a mine while on her way to Turuga. Several rescue ships hurried to the spot immediately upon the receipt of the S. O. S.

II

Announcement by the Board of Information Made on November 6, 1941

It is clear from the wireless messages sent out by the Kehi Maru and other circumstances that the disaster that befell the vessel was caused by a floating mine which had drifted from within the territorial waters of the Soviet Union.

Therefore, the Vice-Minister for Foreign Affairs, Mr. Haruhiko Nishi, on the afternoon of the 6th, requested the Soviet Ambassador at Tokyo, M. Constantin Smetanin, to call on him at the Foreign Office and presented to the latter the most solemn protest, demanding his Government to make a reply with sincerity.

About this collection

This site gathers the verbatim texts of treaties, laws, speeches and other official documents printed in two English-language periodicals published in wartime Japan: the Tokyo Gazette (1937–1942), a monthly digest of government reports issued under the supervision of the Cabinet Information Bureau, and Contemporary Japan: A Review of Far Eastern Affairs (issues of 1941–1945), published by the Foreign Affairs Association of Japan, whose “Documentary Material” section reprinted official texts.

Only texts printed in full in the journals are included; commentary, summaries and articles about them are not. The issues covered are those indexed on the Tokyo Gazette table of contents and Contemporary Japan pages at Frog in a Well, and scanned on the Internet Archive.

These are official translations and statements of the wartime Japanese state and its allies, and should be read as such.
