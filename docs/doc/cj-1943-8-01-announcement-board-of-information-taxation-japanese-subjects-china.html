p. 1084

In pursuance of the basic purpose of the Joint Declaration of Japan and China concerning Co-operation for the Prosecution of the War and in conformity with the stipulations of the Agreement between Japan and China concerning the Retrocession of Concessions and Relinquishment of Extraterritorial Rights, both which were signed on January 9 of this year, the Japanese Government had, as the first step with reference to the extraterritorial rights enjoyed by Japan in China, their Ambassador to China carry on negotiations with the Foreign Minister of the National Government of China concerning a draft treaty based upon a concrete plan deliberated upon and formulated by the Technical Commission mentioned in Article 6 of the said Agreement which relates to the application of the laws and regulations of China relative to taxation on Japanese subjects. Satisfactory agreement on the draft having been reached as a result of the negotiations, the Treaty between Japan and China concerning Taxation on Japanese subjects, the Supplementary Agreement to the Treaty and the Terms of Understanding relating thereto between the Japanese and Chinese Plenipotentiaries were signed at Nanking today, July 31, 1943.

About this collection

This site gathers the verbatim texts of treaties, laws, speeches and other official documents printed in two English-language periodicals published in wartime Japan: the Tokyo Gazette (1937–1942), a monthly digest of government reports issued under the supervision of the Cabinet Information Bureau, and Contemporary Japan: A Review of Far Eastern Affairs (issues of 1941–1945), published by the Foreign Affairs Association of Japan, whose “Documentary Material” section reprinted official texts.

It also includes the “Texts of Treaties, Agreements, Notes and Statutes of the Powers Concerning Manchuria” (1895–1932) printed as Supplement III of the Japan-Manchoukuo Year Book 1934, and, from the two periodicals, selected government programmes, reports, statistical articles, chronologies and profiles of public figures.

Only texts printed in full in the journals are included; commentary, summaries and articles about them are not. The issues covered are those indexed on the Tokyo Gazette table of contents and Contemporary Japan pages at Frog in a Well, and scanned on the Internet Archive.

These are official translations and statements of the wartime Japanese state and its allies, and should be read as such.
