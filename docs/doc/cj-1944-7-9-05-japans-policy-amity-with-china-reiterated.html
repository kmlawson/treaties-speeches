Japan's Policy for Amity with China Reiterated: Statement by the Government, July 5, 1944

p. 838

Since the outbreak of the war of greater East Asia, Japan, actuated solely by her earnest desire for the liberation and stabilization of greater East Asia and for the establishment of a new world order, has been fighting vigorously to defeat the evil designs of America and Britain in co-operation with the various countries and peoples of the region and in close collaboration with her gallant allies in Europe.

The war situation has now entered upon a decisive stage both in the East and in the West. In the Pacific, the counter-offensives of the enemy have increased in intensity, while the Imperial Japanese forces are exerting

p. 839

their utmost efforts to frustrate them completely. On the Chinese continent, on the other hand, American and Britain are manifesting even more nakedly their sinister design of aggression and domination. By taking the initative [sic], however, the Imperial Japanese forces have captured enemy bases in various areas and are everywhere dealing shattering blows to the hostile forces.

The aim of our military operations in China, now in progress, being simply to frustrate the attempts of America and Britain at aggression and domination, the Chinese people are, it goes without saying, our friends and even the armed forces under the Chungking régime who are opposed to co-operation with America and Britain are not our enemy.

What Japan earnestly desires is to bring to consummation a permanent relationship of good-neighbourliness and friendship between Japan and China on the basis of respect for sovereignty and independence in conformity with the Japanese-Chinese Pact of Alliance and the Joint Declaration of the Greater East Asiatic Nations.

In the face of the present decisive war situation, Japan, by more than ever demonstrating the true character and spirit of the Empire, is determined to continue her utmost efforts to attain the ultimate victory.

About this collection

This site gathers the verbatim texts of treaties, laws, speeches and other official documents printed in two English-language periodicals published in wartime Japan: the Tokyo Gazette (1937–1942), a monthly digest of government reports issued under the supervision of the Cabinet Information Bureau, and Contemporary Japan: A Review of Far Eastern Affairs (issues of 1941–1945), published by the Foreign Affairs Association of Japan, whose “Documentary Material” section reprinted official texts.

It also includes the “Texts of Treaties, Agreements, Notes and Statutes of the Powers Concerning Manchuria” (1895–1932) printed as Supplement III of the Japan-Manchoukuo Year Book 1934 (the first of a section of texts from year books), and, from the two periodicals, selected government programmes, reports, statistical articles, chronologies and profiles of public figures.

Only texts printed in full in the journals are included; commentary, summaries and articles about them are not. The issues covered are those indexed on the Tokyo Gazette table of contents and Contemporary Japan pages at Frog in a Well, and scanned on the Internet Archive.

These are official translations and statements of the wartime Japanese state and its allies, and should be read as such.
