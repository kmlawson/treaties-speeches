Ordinance Concerning the Effective Date of the Judicial Protective Work Law; Organization of the Judicial Protective Work Commission; Ordinance Concerning the Committee for Judicial Protection (Imperial Ordinances Nos. 642, 643 and 644 of Sept. 13, 1939)

p. 201

Sept. 14, 1939 is set as the effective date of the Judicial Protective Work Law. The Judicial Protective Work Commission, under the supervision of the Minister of Justice, which is to be composed of a president (the Minister of Justice) and no more than 15 members, has been organized. The competent Minister is authorized, on recommendation of this Commission, to withdraw permission for operating judicial protective work or restrict their operation, if those operating such works in accordance with the provisions of Article 7 of the Law should violate the Law, injure or be suspected of injuring public welfare, or commit indefensible irregularities. The Committee for Judicial Protection has been organized for the purpose of protecting those mentioned under Article 1 of the Law—namely, those discharged from public prosecution with no supplementary suit brought against them, those under a sentence of conditional condemnation, those under suspension of sentence, those exempted from execution of sentence, those released on parole, those who have served out their term, and those who have been under judicial protection in accordance with the

p. 202

Juvenile Law. This Committee, under the supervision of the Minister of Justice, is to investigate the attitude, environment and circumstances of those under protection, with a view to reshaping their character and assuring their livelihood. Other provisions also have been made in connection with the foregoing.

About this collection

This site gathers the verbatim texts of treaties, laws, speeches and other official documents printed in two English-language periodicals published in wartime Japan: the Tokyo Gazette (1937–1942), a monthly digest of government reports issued under the supervision of the Cabinet Information Bureau, and Contemporary Japan: A Review of Far Eastern Affairs (issues of 1941–1945), published by the Foreign Affairs Association of Japan, whose “Documentary Material” section reprinted official texts.

It also includes the “Texts of Treaties, Agreements, Notes and Statutes of the Powers Concerning Manchuria” (1895–1932) printed as Supplement III of the Japan-Manchoukuo Year Book 1934 (the first of a section of texts from year books), and, from the two periodicals, selected government programmes, reports, statistical articles, chronologies and profiles of public figures.

Only texts printed in full in the journals are included; commentary, summaries and articles about them are not. The issues covered are those indexed on the Tokyo Gazette table of contents and Contemporary Japan pages at Frog in a Well, and scanned on the Internet Archive.

These are official translations and statements of the wartime Japanese state and its allies, and should be read as such.
