Statement by the Foreign Office concerning the Conclusion of the Agreement on Cultural Cooperation between Japan and Germany

Type
Document
Date
25 November 1938
Speaker / issued by
Foreign Office
Printed in
Tokyo Gazette, January, 1939, pp. 29-30
Section
Agreement on Cultural Cooperation between Japan and Germany and Statements of the Foreign Office
View the scan on the Internet Archive →
p. 29

The cultural relations between Japan and Germany in the domains of medicine, jurisprudence, literature, music, etc. have always been particularly close. In the recent past the general relations of the two countries to each other have become even closer especially since the conclusion of the Anti-Comintern Pact.

When in September this year the German Government proposed to the Japanese Government the conclusion of an agreement upon which the further consolidation and expansion of their cultural relations should be based, the Japanese Government gladly accepted the proposal. The conclusion, after a short negotiation, of the agreement on cultural cooperation concluded and put into force today is heartily welcomed as another token of the friendly relations so happily connecting Japan and Germany. The agreement is the first of its kind put into force by Japan. It clearly affirms in its preamble that the characteristic features of the cultures of both countries shall be the basis of the future cooperation of the two Governments in promoting their cultural relations. The agreement itself gives the general principles to be acted upon. Within its scope of the many questions to be taken up, the competent authorities are to come by mutual agreement to determinations on the following points :

1. Establishment of committees for cultural work. 2. Maintenance and expansion of cultural institutions. 3. Recommendation of teachers.

p. 30

4. Facilities for Government students. 5. Exchange of professors and students. 6. Promotion of friendly relations between organizations of young people. 7. Favourable treatment of schools. 8. Exchange of books and periodicals. 9. Exchanges in the realm of art. 10. Exchange of films. 11. Exchange of broadcasts. 12. Exchanges in the fields of sport and hygiene.

About this collection

This site gathers the verbatim texts of treaties, laws, speeches and other official documents printed in two English-language periodicals published in wartime Japan: the Tokyo Gazette (1937–1942), a monthly digest of government reports issued under the supervision of the Cabinet Information Bureau, and Contemporary Japan: A Review of Far Eastern Affairs (issues of 1941–1945), published by the Foreign Affairs Association of Japan, whose “Documentary Material” section reprinted official texts.

Only texts printed in full in the journals are included; commentary, summaries and articles about them are not. The issues covered are those indexed on the Tokyo Gazette table of contents and Contemporary Japan pages at Frog in a Well, and scanned on the Internet Archive.

These are official translations and statements of the wartime Japanese state and its allies, and should be read as such.

Transcription. The texts were transcribed from the page scans by a large language model reading the page images, and have not been proofread by a person. Original spelling and romanisation are kept. Page numbers in the margin link to the page of the scan on the Internet Archive: check the scan before quoting.
